Three kinds of demand

We use "demand" in three senses and keep them apart. Token demand is the capital that holds a subnet's alpha (its own token), and the Emission Gate rewards it. Paying-customer demand is revenue from people buying what a subnet produces. Revenue reaching the token is customer money that buys, burns or locks alpha. The 2026 rules reward the first kind. This report tests whether the second and third follow.

What it means for $TAO

Bittensor is a network of independent markets called subnets. Each one produces a single thing, such as inference, GPU time or drug candidates, and pays its contributors from new $TAO. Until 2026 a subnet could earn largely by existing. Since July, its emissions scale with the capital its token attracts.

For holders, the question has moved from how much $TAO gets issued to what that issuance buys. If revenue keeps compounding while halvings cut issuance, emissions are buying real demand. If revenue stalls, the 2026 rules have only rearranged a subsidy. Slide 21 sets out what would settle it.

Why these five

Each one shows a different route from emissions to demand. Metanova and Carbon sell scientific results. Engy competes in inference, the network's biggest and most crowded revenue category. Vanta sells into a Web2 industry. REAX is a product subnet at its earliest stage, with customers but no route to its token yet.

The set is hand-picked, not a sample, so the scorecard describes these five subnets, not the network. In February, Bittensor: The Intelligence Olympics picked subnets for enterprise traction. This report applies a stricter filter, because under the 2026 rules a subnet's business has to reach its token. That is why "How value reaches alpha" is the first column.

01

The protocol in 2026

Bittensor's economics were rewritten in the second half of 2026. This section covers the releases, what the halving did to supply, and the two changes that matter most: Root Reborn and the Emission Gate.

Before July, a subnet could steer its own emissions by moving its own token. Price-driven emissions, the Emission Gate and Root Reborn closed that route together, so a subnet now has to attract capital that wants to stay. The pace was fast for a network holding this much value. Each release shipped while subnets, validators and stakers were still adjusting to the last, which is why governance is on the risk list (slide 21).

Against the 21M max supply on Oct 11, 2026, 35.5% was staked, 19.8% was issued but unstaked and 44.7% was not yet issued.

The halving raised the stakes of the new rules. Since July they have divided half the pre-halving issuance among the same number of slots, so a subnet's share is worth more to win and easier to lose. With about 64% of issued $TAO staked, where existing stake goes also shapes the network more than new issuance does. That is why Root Reborn matters.

Root used to be the network's parking lot. It holds nearly half of all $TAO, and stakers who didn't want to pick subnets collected a dividend there, with no say in where their capital went. Now that dividend arrives as alpha baskets. Validators curate each basket by picking the subnets that go into it, the alpha is held until it is claimable, and stakers collect it through root claims (v448). Passive stake has become a vote on subnets, and validators act more like fund managers. A subnet now has to win them over as well as retail. For how staking worked before 2026, see Bittensor: The Investment History.

eTAO (v481) then fixed how every subnet splits its emissions. The owner takes between 0 and 33%, validators take up to 50%, and miners get at least 33%.

The change was contested. At least one validator publicly warned that Root Reborn concentrates allocation power (LBank News; OurCryptoTalk).

No change this year mattered more to subnet teams. Under TaoFlow, the previous model, a subnet could cycle its own token to move its emission share. Now demand for the token sets most of its emissions, along a smooth curve, s³ ÷ (s³ + θ³). Here s is a subnet's smoothed price share after the miner-burn penalty, and θ is the bar, recalculated every 360 blocks. By default θ lands around the 32nd-ranked subnet, where a subnet keeps half its linear share, and root can adjust it. Subnets with real users, revenue or a credible path to either can hold capital. Subnets that relied on incentive games fade below the bar.

The burn penalty closes an obvious loophole. Burning miner emissions used to make a subnet look scarcer at no cost. Now it also cuts the subnet's share, so teams have to choose between scarcity and rewards.

Ranking mid-table used to pay. Now a subnet clears the bar or watches its emissions fade. Concentration is the intended result, but it doesn't prove the right subnets won. That is why the spotlights judge each subnet first by how value reaches its alpha.

Gamma credits matter most for the revenue story, because buyers would burn a subnet's tokens for dollar-valued credits and then spend those credits on that subnet's work. Null Consensus lets subnets with checkable results, such as benchmarks and competitions, run far more miners, but it puts scoring in fewer hands. Shorting helps price discovery. Today a subnet's alpha can only be bought or ignored, so overvalued subnets correct slowly.

02

Where the money goes

Protocol changes matter only if behaviour follows. This section asks how much subnets earn, which ones earn it, and how that compares with their emissions.

On this source, growth is speeding up. The last step, from $50M+ to ~$70M, took about a month.

Three limits apply. SubConnect builds its estimates from off-chain payment records, so they can't be checked on-chain and part of the input is self-reported. Its first index, on Aug 26, covered 24 subnets and put their revenue at $28M to $35M. If coverage widened between readings, part of the rise is coverage, not new revenue. And the series starts after the July changes, so there is no before and after. The growth fits what the new rules were built to produce, but it doesn't prove they caused it.

We use one series on purpose, because methods differ widely. BlockEden, using a different method, put revenue at $43M on May 3, above SubConnect's ~$35M three months later. Measured from SubConnect's $28M lower bound, the rise would be 2.5×.

Revenue clusters in compute because the market pays first for what it already understands: GPU time and cheap inference, which have the clearest buyers and the easiest price comparisons. On SubConnect's Nov 4 index, compute and infrastructure took 78% to 82% of estimated subnet revenue (via CryptoBriefing), and that is a share of revenue, not of emissions. Lium reported $2.32M in October renter spend. At its published 5% fee, that implies about $116K in October platform fees and $1.39M annualized at the same pace.

Inference is getting crowded, though. Many subnets serve the same open models, so the open question is who keeps the margin. The likely winners pair cheap, emission-funded supply with something customers can't easily get elsewhere: reliability, privacy or verification.

In August and early September, the dollar value of new TAO entering Chutes’ pool was about seven times its estimated customer revenue. Chutes’ costs would be needed to assess profitability.

The two sides count different things. The emissions side is the $TAO the network sent to Chutes' liquidity pool, worth ~$32.4M annualized over Aug 10 to Sep 6, 2026. Its owner, validators and miners are paid separately, in Chutes' alpha. The revenue side, $4.54M annualized over Aug 8 to Sep 6, is what Chutes reports through its own API, which DefiLlama reads. That series ends on Sep 6, so we can't see revenue after that. The 5.9:1 sensitivity applies the lower Nov 9 issuance share to the same August–September revenue estimate. The data contains no November revenue observation.

Watch whether Chutes’ revenue grows and how its share of TAO issuance changes. Future halvings reduce network issuance in TAO terms. The value of that issuance in dollars also depends on the TAO price. A subnet that earns dollars has to buy $TAO on the open market before it can buy back its own alpha, so dollar revenue becomes demand for $TAO. In a buyback and burn, that alpha is then destroyed, shrinking supply for every holder. That is confirmed at Engy (slide 15), while Vanta documents a route but doesn't disclose how much of its burn customers fund (slide 16).

03

Subnet spotlights

We judge each subnet first by one question: how does value reach its alpha? Users aren't enough if nothing links them to the token. The research bets, Metanova and Carbon, come first. Next are Engy and Vanta, which route customer money to their tokens, then REAX, which has customers but no route yet. Every spotlight follows the same rubric, from strategic positioning to what to watch for, and a map of the wider field closes the section.

Elsewhere on the network, buybacks are the common answer. SubConnect counts 14+ revenue subnets funding them (via CryptoBriefing, Nov 4, 2026), and Synth announced a $100K buyback programme over eight weeks and reported a first purchase of 9,965.64 SN50 alpha. Owner-share decisions went both ways: On Oct 21, Almanac increased its miner share from 41% to 50%, and SN35, Epago and Consted 2.0 set their owner shares to 33%.

Buybacks are one lens, not the scorecard. A buyback recycles revenue, so it only grades subnets that already sell something. Research subnets are valued like a biotech pipeline before sales, and every one of them would fail that test. As our livestream with Metanova put it, drug discovery is an expected-value bet whose payoff takes years (Khala Research × Metanova, Oct 23, 2026). So we use two scorecards.

The research scorecard isn't a softer test. Milestones are harder to fake and slower to arrive, and a research subnet can pass every one and still never route value to its token.

Strategic positioning. Metanova is Bittensor's most advanced drug-discovery bet, and this year it moved from screens to physical lab work. On our livestream, co-founders Micaela Bazo and Pedro Penna described an open platform that also builds its own drug assets and follows the usual biotech route: patent the IP, then license or partner (Khala Research × Metanova, Oct 23, 2026). If the results hold, it would be Bittensor's clearest proof of value outside software.

Mechanism. Metanova runs drug discovery as open competitions, around the clock, and pays whoever finds the best candidate. It began with single-target screening and now runs three competitions. Per the team, searchable chemical space is 678.5 billion possible molecules, scored with Boltz-2, and a winner must beat the incumbent across four targets.

Competitive landscape. Isomorphic and Recursion work behind closed doors with selected partners, and Isomorphic's pharma deals have been reported at over $3B (TAO Daily, Jul 9, 2026). On Bittensor, Niome and Minos (slide 18) work in genomics, not drug candidates, so they are neighbours rather than rivals.

Structural advantages. Open competition draws on more searchers than one lab can hire. The ONEPOT.AI partnership (May) lets winning compounds reach robotic synthesis within days. Yalotein began producing nanobodies, a type of small antibody, in July and is taking 50 candidates into in-vitro testing. At the Exploit Summit, Metanova showed first lab data: per the team, most of the miner-inspired small molecules had a longer half-life than the recently FDA-approved drugs they were benchmarked against. That is early and says nothing yet about efficacy (TAO Daily, Oct 24, 2026).

How value reaches alpha. Today, through alignment. The team cut the miner burn to 0% on Jun 24, citing the new incentives. It pays miners only when a submission beats the current best and says the owner share locks automatically under Bittensor’s conviction mechanism. Holders get team commitment but no customer money yet. A route could come later through licensing-funded buybacks, outside labs paying to run targets, or stakers directing emissions to SN68 because its output is validated. None exists today, and this is not a forecast.

Risk factors. Metanova has the most tangible science among our spotlights and the longest road to its token. Drug timelines run in years and most candidates fail. There is no licensing deal yet, let alone one whose terms reach the token.

Takeaway. Metanova has moved from screening to lab work. The next tests are whether a candidate meets its lab criteria and whether licensing proceeds can reach SN68.

Strategic positioning. On Nov 9, Bittensor.ai reported zero TAO injected into Carbon’s pool and 7,200 alpha emitted. Carbon separately describes customer-funded Model Discovery challenges, with rewards for verified improvements.

Mechanism. Carbon sells the checking of physics surrogates, which are AI models that approximate expensive simulations and run far faster. Miners submit recipes rather than trained weights. Validators rebuild every model in a fixed environment and test it on data the miner never saw. Battery fast charging and ageing come first, then AI-chip cold plates, electric motors and silicon photonics.

Competitive landscape. Carbon doesn't sell a better simulator than commercial software or in-house teams. It sells evidence that a fast model can be trusted.

Structural advantages. Engineers won't stake a battery or chip-cooling design on a model nobody has checked. Recipes make every result reproducible, and testing on hidden data plus a physics check makes overfitting hard.

How value reaches alpha. Planned only, through buybacks funded by company revenue. Nothing reaches the token today.

Risk factors. Carbon still needs to demonstrate paid commercial use. Most detailed coverage so far comes from an AI-generated news piece and a third-party report that declares a conflict of interest. The first-week rally came before any revenue.

Takeaway. Carbon’s product is evidence an engineer can use. A paid audit with a disclosed outcome or a completed customer-funded challenge would be the first commercial proof.

Strategic positioning. Engy is the spotlight closest to the thesis. Customers pay, the revenue burns alpha, and miners are scored only on paid work.

Mechanism. Model weights are pinned by a published hash, every response carries an activation fingerprint, and sampled recompute audits are rolling out. A miner's score is the number of billed tokens it served on paid, successful requests.

Competitive landscape. Centralized APIs serve the same open models but can only ask customers to trust them. On Bittensor, Engy competes with Chutes and other inference subnets (slide 19).

Structural advantages. Customers can check that they got the model they paid for, without trusting special hardware. SubConnect named Engy, alongside Lium and Targon, as the main drivers of the jump in subnet revenue to $50M+. The founder's demo of a 2.8-trillion-parameter model running on 80 consumer GPUs passed 750,000 views on X.

How value reaches alpha. Confirmed, and it is the cleanest route in this report.

Risk factors. The full audit system is still rolling out, and it will decide whether verification becomes a lasting edge. An average price cut of about 30% was planned for October 2026. A cut can win volume but also squeeze the revenue that funds the burn.

Takeaway. Engy has the clearest customer-to-alpha route in the report. The next test is whether paid usage and revenue after the price cut support recurring burns.

Strategic positioning. Vanta sells Bittensor infrastructure into a Web2 industry, and it answered both big 2026 changes. The Kraken listing in July brought demand from outside Bittensor's own markets. In October, when eTAO let owners choose their share, Vanta set its cut to zero.

Mechanism. Validators score miners' futures signals on risk-adjusted returns, with strict drawdown limits. The bet is that holders, not the team, should capture the value.

Competitive landscape. Retail prop trading is a large and opaque industry. Many firms earn mainly from evaluation fees and are known for avoiding payouts. Vanta competes on public, rule-based scoring.

Structural advantages. Transparent scoring, an owner share of zero, and a Kraken spot listing since Jul 16, 2026.

How value reaches alpha. Partly documented. A trader pays cash for an account, the entity miner that sold it buys alpha on the market, and part of that alpha is burned at registration (vantanetwork.io/theta). Other burns come from failed traders' forfeited collateral and from unclaimed emissions (Taoshi SN8 litepaper). Vanta hasn’t disclosed how much of the alpha burned was funded by customer fees.

Risk factors. Public sources also describe collateral and payouts differently, so treat the product details as provisional.

Takeaway. Vanta shows how evaluation fees can reach alpha. A dated breakdown of burns by funding source would show how much the evaluation business contributes.

Strategic positioning. REAX is a product subnet at its earliest stage, so it is judged on revenue, not milestones. Mainnet went live on Oct 14, 2026, with pricing and, the team says, paying customers from day one (The TAO Daily, Oct 17, 2026).

Mechanism. A decision model answers a finite-choice question, such as which route to take, whether to escalate or how to score. It gives a probability for each option instead of free text. Miners host pinned, approved models. Validators recompute answers about every two minutes and score fidelity first, then speed, then verified capacity. Customers buy through decisionmodels.io, billed in fiat and kept separate from the chain's score ledger.

Competitive landscape. The REAX API is compatible with TypeSafe's closed Jev service. REAX 1.3, an open model, claims #1 on the Hugging Face Decision Index, ahead of the closed model that set the benchmark. On Bittensor, Zils (SN127) also sits in the category (slide 19).

Structural advantages. REAX bills customers in fiat and ties rewards to demand: since Nov 2, 30% of miner rewards have followed measured customer demand (reax.co/status). The founder says one organic customer sent 1.7 million requests in a single week.

How value reaches alpha. Not yet through revenue. tao.eco shows τ0 in buybacks since Oct 22, so what burns today is unearned emission, not customer money buying alpha.

Risk factors. No revenue figure is disclosed, and the subnet is small. It has been on mainnet four weeks, and its competition tracks have been paused since Oct 30.

Takeaway. REAX ties part of miner rewards to measured demand. Repeat paid usage and a traceable route from API revenue to alpha are the next tests.

What to watch

Where the network stands, and what would prove the thesis right or wrong.

Revenue rising, ~2× in ten weeks: ~$35M (Aug 26; first index range $28M to $35M) to ~$70M annualized (Nov 4, 2026) on the SubConnect index

Issuance halved: ~7,200 $TAO a day before the Dec 15, 2025 halving, ~3,600 since

Network-wide, ~4:1: ~3,600 $TAO a day × 365 × $219 ≈ $288M of annual issuance vs ~$70M of estimated revenue (Nov 2026)

Chutes’ pool issuance vs revenue, ~7:1: ~$32.4M of $TAO issuance to its pool vs $4.54M of revenue, annualized, using Aug 10 to Sep 6 for issuance and Aug 8 to Sep 6 for revenue

The four numbers move differently. Revenue moves with customers, while issuance in $TAO falls only at halvings. The network ratio moves with both, and with the $TAO price. Chutes shows the ratio at a single subnet, where it is worse than across the network.

In 2027, look for repeat customer spend and signs that revenue is growing relative to the value of TAO issued.

Our view

Bittensor is becoming an exchange that commissions and prices intelligence, and the value should sit in that market rather than in any single model. It resembles the early web's open protocols, except this time the protocol comes with an asset you can own. Today the network issues about $4 of $TAO for every $1 of estimated revenue, and at Chutes, on overlapping August–September windows, about $7 reached its pool.

That gap is the asymmetry. After the next halving, at today's price, revenue of roughly $144M a year would match issuance. That is one more doubling. If revenue keeps compounding on a consistent index, $TAO's emissions are buying real demand. If it stalls, the 2026 rules will only have moved the subsidy around.

Buybacks are the right test for product subnets, not research subnets. Research subnets have to produce results that hold up outside the network, and for Metanova, 2027 lab data will say more than any buyback.

A first way to bet against a subnet

In November, Gamma gave customers a way to pay for a subnet’s services using credits created by burning its token.

What changed in practice

July’s release-note simulation raised the top eight’s emission share from 32.8% to 52.7%. They received 57.0% on November 10. Live data begins after the upgrade, so it cannot establish the before-and-after change. July simulation · Daily emissions series

SubConnect’s annualized revenue estimate doubled from ~$35M in August to ~$70M in November. Expanded coverage may explain part of the rise; the upgrades’ contribution is unproven.

At an illustrative $219 per TAO, annualized issuance was still worth roughly four times the November revenue estimate.

The five deep dives ask who pays for what subnets build and where that money goes.

Protocol release notes and TAO supply

Conviction. Locking a subnet’s alpha builds conviction over time. Once the subnet is at least a year old, ownership can transfer if the highest-conviction hotkey exceeds 18% of the subnet’s eligible alpha. Protocol rules.

Emission Gate mechanics. The gate scales emissions using s³ ÷ (s³ + θ³), where s is the subnet’s smoothed price share after the miner-burn penalty and θ is the threshold, recalculated every 360 blocks. At the threshold a subnet keeps half its linear share. Root can adjust the threshold.

Catalysts & Risk Factors

The 2026 changes tied emissions more closely to demand for subnet tokens. The next test is whether the services those subnets build attract enough paying customers to narrow the gap between revenue and issuance.

Into 2027

Can customer revenue catch up with issuance?

The signals below track repeat customer demand and the risks that could slow its growth.

Signs of progress
  1. SubConnect reports higher revenue across a comparable set of subnets.
  2. Customers pay for subnet services with Gamma credits and come back to spend again.
  3. Chutes discloses higher customer revenue in a comparable reporting window.
  4. Subnets outside compute earn repeat revenue from unaffiliated customers. For research subnets, progress means milestones such as Metanova’s in-vitro results.
  5. More subnets route customer revenue into buybacks, as Engy does.
  6. Demand-tied rewards spread beyond compute, as at REAX.
Risks to watch
  1. Customer revenue grows more slowly than the dollar value of TAO issuance.
  2. Concentration: revenue stays in compute, where inference is crowded.
  3. Governance: Root Reborn and Null Consensus put allocation and scoring in fewer hands.
  4. Builder exit: teams below the bar have less reason to stay.
  5. Liquidity: alpha markets are thin, so burns and exits can move prices sharply.
What would change our mind
  • The revenue index flattens or falls over the next two readings.
  • Issuance per dollar of revenue rises on matched periods while the $TAO price holds.
  • No non-compute subnet discloses repeat revenue from unaffiliated customers in 2027 (token link tracked separately).

04

Closing Thesis

The July reforms changed how Bittensor allocates emissions. Subnets now have a stronger incentive to attract demand for their tokens. Whether they can turn those incentives into products customers pay for remains the larger question.

The five deep dives show why it’s worth following the money. Engy provides the clearest example of customer revenue funding an alpha burn. Vanta documents a route from fees to burns, but the customer-funded share remains undisclosed. REAX shows that customer demand can shape miner rewards even before payments reach alpha. Research subnets have a different milestone. For Metanova, partner lab testing is progress, and the next test is whether a candidate meets the lab criteria for its intended use.

At the TAO price used in this report, the next halving would bring annual issuance down to roughly $144M, about twice SubConnect’s latest revenue estimate. The halving can narrow that gap on its own. Evidence of a stronger network would come from repeat customer spending across a comparable set of subnets, more cases where payments reach alpha, and research results that independent partners can validate.

Method and Data Notes

Snapshot dates. Each figure is dated where it appears; protocol dates are month-level.

Chutes issuance and revenue. 11.3% average daily share of network emission, Aug 10 to Sep 6, 2026 (bittensor.ai daily series) × ~3,600 $TAO/day × 365 × $218.2 average $TAO price (CoinGecko) ≈ $32.4M. Revenue: Chutes API via DefiLlama, annualized through Sep 6. Sensitivity at the same historical revenue run rate: 9.35% (bittensor.ai, Nov 9, 2026) × $219 ≈ $26.9M. Second tracker: Own Your Mind's chain index shows Chutes at 6.55% on Aug 15 and 5.82% on Sep 22, 2026, roughly half the bittensor.ai series; 6.55% gives ≈ $18.8M, or ~4.1:1. The trackers give different emission shares. The revenue window starts two days before the issuance window.

Four kinds of revenue figure, never added together.

  • Index estimate: SubConnect's annualized estimate from off-chain payment records, used for the network total, trend and compute's share. "Verified" is SubConnect's term, as reported by TokenPost; we found no published definition.
  • Company-reported, read by a tracker: Chutes' revenue API via DefiLlama's adapter, a 30-day window, annualized. The endpoint now requires authentication.
  • Company-reported: October renter spend and published fee terms for Lium, plus dated figures from Engy, Vanta, Metanova and REAX.
  • Other third-party: BlockEden, context only.

Khala calculations compare issuance value with revenue, not costs or profit. Chutes’ ratio is ~7.1:1 using bittensor.ai (~4.1:1 on the alternative tracker); the network ratio is ~4.1:1. At $219/TAO, post-halving issuance would annualize to ~$144M. bittensor.ai

Not measured: subnet costs, margins, outside-customer share, burn effects on prices, or revenue outside SubConnect’s coverage. Chutes’ revenue series ends September 6; daily concentration data begins after the Emission Gate launch.
